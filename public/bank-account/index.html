Business bank account walkthroughWatch lesson

Why this matters

Banking history shows stability, cash flow, and whether the business can handle credit.

This step helps the member open and use the business bank account under the exact legal name so deposits, balances, and banking history support future applications.

Choose your banking relationship

Start with a relationship bank, not just a checking account.

The goal is to open the business account at a bank or credit union that can support the company beyond deposits. Look for a real business banker, clean account handling, future credit conversations, and lending options that fit the business.

Best first choice

Local or community bank

Often better for early relationship lending because you may be able to speak with a business banker and build a track record over time.

Good when available

Strong regional bank

Useful when the bank has business bankers, local branches, business credit products, and a reasonable path to future lending.

Use carefully

Large national bank

Convenient for branches and technology, but underwriting can be more automated and less relationship-based for newer businesses.

Ask first

Business credit support

Before opening the account, ask whether they offer business lines of credit, secured loans, SBA lending, business credit cards, and a named banker.

Ask before opening

Will this account be opened under the exact legal business name, EIN, address, and phone number?Will I have access to a business banker or only general customer service?Does the institution offer business credit cards, lines of credit, secured loans, or SBA lending?What minimum balance, activity, or account age matters before credit conversations?

Banking foundation warning

The bank account is not just for deposits. It becomes a relationship and stability signal.

Lenders look for a real operating account with consistent activity, clean handling, and enough balance history to support future applications. Whenever possible, start with a local community bank or regional bank where you can build a relationship with a business banker.

Open the account in the exact legal business name with the same address, phone, and EIN from prior lessons.Choose relationship banking over convenience alone; a local banker may understand the business better when it is time to discuss credit.Local and community banks can sometimes move faster on small-business loans because the decision process may be closer to the customer.Avoid overdrafts, returned items, rapid account changes, and low-balance history before major credit applications.

Bring these documents

Prepare the bank visit before you apply.

Formation document or Articles of Organization/Incorporation.EIN confirmation letter from the IRS.Operating agreement, bylaws, or ownership authorization if the bank asks.Owner identification, business address proof, and opening deposit.
